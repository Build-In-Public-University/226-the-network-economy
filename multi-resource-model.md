# Capitalism, labor-time coordination, and the proposed network economy

## Scope

This is an analytic comparison, not a claim that every historical communist or capitalist system optimized one variable. The simplified baselines are useful because they expose what each accounting regime makes visible and what it tends to push outside the ledger.

## The three models

### Communism as labor/time coordination

Idealized objective:

```text
minimize socially necessary labor time
subject to collective needs and available productive capacity
```

What it makes visible:

- labor requirements;
- working time;
- productive capacity;
- direct allocation of resources;
- shared provision.

What can disappear:

- local and tacit information;
- preference heterogeneity;
- dissent and political coercion;
- administrative and care labor inside the collective;
- the cost of revising a plan;
- exit and contestability.

The core danger is labor-time reductionism: time is more socially legible than money, but it is not the whole of value, freedom, or knowledge.

### Capitalism as money-return coordination

Idealized objective:

```text
maximize monetary return / liquidity / asset value
subject to property, contract, and solvency constraints
```

What it makes visible:

- purchasing power;
- prices;
- returns;
- liquidity;
- some dispersed information;
- private willingness to pay.

What can disappear:

- unpaid care;
- ecological recovery;
- public infrastructure;
- displaced coordination work;
- future option loss;
- burdens that land on actors without purchasing power;
- costs that arrive after the decision-maker has exited.

The core danger is money reductionism: money is a powerful coordination signal, but it can reward an action while its repair burden is sent elsewhere in the network or forward in time.

### Network economy as relationship coordination

Proposed objective:

```text
preserve and improve the relationship among
money, time, energy, information, and future option sets
```

This is not merely capitalism with four more columns. The object being priced is the relationship itself:

- how much money is spent to save whose time;
- how much energy is used to create or preserve information;
- how information changes future coordination cost;
- who receives the benefit;
- who pays the delayed burden;
- whether future options remain open;
- whether the affected nodes can contest or exit the arrangement.

The network economy therefore needs a network-temporal accounting layer, not just a bigger price tag.

## How this changes the SEP arguments

### Marx

Marx's accumulation mechanism becomes one possible source of runaway behavior:

```text
money return → reinvestment pressure → throughput → deferred burden → future dependence
```

The network model asks whether the burden is visible to the node booking the return. If not, it records a time-violence residual rather than assuming the market price is the full signal.

### Hayek and Friedman

The network model does not reject decentralized prices. It asks whether the price channel carries the information that matters:

```text
price-visible information
vs.
network-temporal information
```

A market remains defensible if it can incorporate delayed and distributed costs early enough to alter behavior. If it cannot, local efficiency is not global efficiency.

### Polanyi

Polanyi's fictitious commodities are early examples of a relationship being forced into a single exchange metric. Land, labor, and money are not ordinary commodities because they carry social, human, and institutional relations that the transaction price cannot exhaust.

The network economy generalizes this insight: time, energy, and information also have relational states that are not captured by a single local price.

### Keynes

Keynes supplies the short-horizon and liquidity mechanism. A liquid return can be privately rational while starving long-term productive or restorative capacity.

The network model adds an explicit future option-set term so that a present reward cannot silently consume the conditions required for later agency.

### Rawls and republican critiques

The network model translates these critiques into control variables:

- who sets the objective;
- who controls the ledger;
- who can contest the metric;
- who can exit;
- whose future options are protected;
- whose burdens are treated as acceptable noise.

That matters because a network economy can reproduce the planner problem in a more technical costume. More measurement does not automatically mean more freedom.

## Comparative table

| Dimension | Labor-time coordination | Money-return coordination | Network economy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary signal | socially necessary labor time | price, profit, liquidity | relationship among money, time, energy, information, future options |
| Strongest visibility | work and productive capacity | purchasing power and exchange | distributed burden and delayed feedback, if measurable |
| Main blind spot | tacit knowledge, dissent, preference, exit | unpaid and delayed costs | measurement error, governance capture, scope explosion |
| Typical runaway mode | plan expansion and bureaucratic lock-in | accumulation and externalized repair | metric expansion, surveillance, false precision |
| Freedom risk | collective coercion | dependence and unequal exit | technocratic governance of the ledger |
| Correction | participation and contestable planning | regulation, redistribution, public provision | multi-objective receipts, adversarial controls, reversible rules |

## A minimal formal model

Let an intervention `a` produce observations across nodes `i` and times `t`:

```text
O(a) = {money_i,t, time_i,t, energy_i,t, information_i,t, option_set_i,t}
```

A local system sees only a projection:

```text
L(a) = money_beneficiary,t0
```

The proposed network-temporal measure is a declared function:

```text
N(a) = F(O(a), network_boundary, time_horizon, weights, consent, exit)
```

A candidate time-violence residual exists when:

```text
N(a) detects a durable burden
and
L(a) does not
```

under a frozen boundary, matched baseline, and preregistered weighting rule.

That last clause prevents the model from becoming an all-purpose accusation machine.

## The future-weighting constraint

The proposed rule is:

```text
future weight ≥ past weight
```

This is best understood as a guardrail against systems that book gains now and export agency loss later. It does not mean every future state must dominate every present need.

The operative comparison should be among four policies:

1. present-biased reward;
2. neutral intertemporal reward;
3. future-protective reward;
4. future-protective reward with uncertainty bounds.

Measure:

- present benefit;
- delayed burden;
- future option-set preservation;
- network distribution;
- reversibility;
- administrative cost;
- surveillance/coercion cost;
- error under boundary perturbation.

## What would make the model worth keeping

The network economy earns distinct status only if it does at least one of these things:

- detects burdens that money-only and labor-time-only ledgers systematically miss;
- predicts delayed repair or exclusion better than those baselines;
- improves allocation under held-out network conditions;
- preserves future options without merely moving coercion into the measurement layer;
- makes tradeoffs legible without claiming false precision.

If it does not, the honest conclusion is that it is a useful vocabulary, not a new economic model.

## Falsifiers

The model should be demoted if:

- money-only or labor-time-only accounting captures the same burdens at lower cost;
- extra dimensions do not improve held-out prediction or allocation;
- apparent gains vanish under time-shuffled, node-shuffled, or boundary-perturbed controls;
- the ledger increases surveillance or administrative labor beyond a declared threshold;
- future-protective weighting produces systematically worse outcomes under a frozen objective.
